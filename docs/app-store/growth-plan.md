## Two-week acquisition experiment

**Days 1–2:** Record the baseline in App Store Connect for one consistent date range, segmented by Search, Browse and referral sources. Capture impressions, product page views, first-time downloads, Apple's reported conversion rate and available retention data. Check price and territory filters. Product page views are useful, but some users download directly from search results, so downloads divided by page views is not a clean funnel for all traffic.

**Days 3–5:** Record three 15–20-second iPhone demos. Each starts with the actual benefit, shows the widget, then briefly shows a connected usage screen. Remove private account information and credentials.

Suggested hooks:
- “Using Claude and Codex? Put your supported usage windows on your iPhone Home Screen.”
- “I built an iPhone widget because I kept checking different AI usage dashboards.”
- “A quick way to keep supported AI usage and API budgets in view.”

**Days 6–10:** Publish through the owner's existing accounts on X, short-form video channels, and relevant AI developer communities that allow self-promotion. Start with places where the owner already participates. Share a working demo and a practical explanation, disclose that it is your app, and answer questions. Do not post repetitive promotional messages across unrelated communities. Use a separate App Store campaign link for each channel, created in App Store Connect.

## Where to invest next

- If relevant traffic sees the listing but rarely downloads: test the first screenshot, clarify supported providers and examine price/setup expectations.
- If conversion is reasonable but impressions remain low: focus on targeted demos, developer communities and searchable content for supported tool combinations.
- If downloads grow but people do not return: prioritize reliable setup, useful widgets and clear notification expectations before paid acquisition.

Keep paid campaigns small until acquisition and retention can be measured. The copy and experiments above are drafts for review.


## Proposed X boost

The owner is considering a boost advertised to deliver roughly 30,000 views. Its cost and audience are not yet known. Views measure exposure; they do not guarantee clicks, iPhone users or downloads. The App Store's 8.86% conversion rate must not be multiplied by X views to forecast installs.

1. Refine the owner's existing post in their voice. Use a specific audience, a visible widget benefit, one short demo and one App Store link. Do not advertise guaranteed real-time background usage alerts.
2. Publish the revised creative organically first and allow a day or two to observe clicks and responses. If testing multiple hooks, treat small differences as directional; organic reach and audience mix vary.
3. Use a distinct App Store Connect campaign link for the paid test. If boosting an existing post, create it with that link beforehand where possible; don't assume the organic and paid traffic can later be separated automatically.
4. Check the boost price, eligible targeting and supported App Store territories. Prefer relevant AI-tool users with iPhones where targeting permits. If the offer provides only broad views with no useful targeting or click reporting, its acquisition value is harder to assess.
5. If the cost is acceptable, start with the smallest useful budget and compare link clicks, attributed first-time downloads and cost per attributed download. Attribution may be delayed or unavailable at low volumes; retain the store's reporting limits when interpreting results.
6. Set a spending limit before paying. At a chosen maximum cost per new downloader, required attributed downloads equal boost cost divided by that maximum cost, rounded up. Decide that acceptable cost from the app's business model rather than an invented industry benchmark.

Prioritize targeted visits over raw view counts. Improve the creative when views produce few clicks; improve the listing or audience match when relevant visits produce few downloads.
